Where we went further than the AI

We didn’t accept “the number of parts matters”. We insisted on testing index + disp on its own: lea rax,[rax*1+0] has only two parts, yet takes 2 cycles on E-cores. Enumerating every combination showed the real rule: index and displacement together.

On AI use: the plan was drafted with an LLM, and it carried outdated Skylake-era rules, which sanity checks and uops.info caught. The AI also explained our result as "three address parts are slow". We didn't accept that; we insisted on testing index plus displacement on its own, and on enumerating every combination. The two-part form lea rax,[rax*1+0] is still slow, which made the real rule clear: index and displacement together. For the extension, the tests, the analysis and these slides were drafted with Claude Code, and every number was checked against the raw data. That check caught one AI error: the write-up said the fast LEA runs 7 or 8 per cycle on Gracemont, but 0.25 cycles per instruction means 4 per cycle. One question from the extension is now answered. Timing couldn’t tell whether the slow form is two micro-ops or one slower micro-op, so we counted micro-ops with Intel VTune: on the Gracemont E-core the slow form is still one micro-op, and it takes 2 cycles. The details are on the last backup slide. One question is still open: the i7-13700H P-core did only 2 loads per cycle in our tests, where Intel documents 3, and we haven’t found out why.

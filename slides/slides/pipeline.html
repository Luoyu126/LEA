This sketches the pipelines our hypothesis predicts, cycle by cycle; the test after the method slide checks it. On the P-core, a three-input adder takes base, index and displacement at once: a 3-to-2 compressor reduces three numbers to two, then one add, all in one cycle, for every unscaled form. Scaling the index by 4 or 8 costs a second cycle on both P-cores, but that is a separate effect. On the E-cores, Gracemont on the second chip as well as Skymont and Crestmont, the adder takes two inputs per cycle, and the index and displacement compete for the same one. Base plus index is added in cycle 1, then the displacement is added in cycle 2. Because the decoder routes by encoding, a zero displacement still takes the second cycle. On the second chip we checked this with Intel VTune’s micro-op counter: the slow form is still one micro-op on Gracemont, so the second cycle happens inside one step, as drawn here, and the instruction is not split in two. That fits the sketch but doesn’t prove how the adder is built.

Hypothesis: two kinds of adder

E-core: a 2-input adder

A ← base

B ← index or disp

↓ one cycle: A + B

Index and displacement share one input. Needing both costs a second step.

P-core: a 3-input adder

base

index

disp

↓ one cycle: 3-to-2, then add

Missing parts are fed in as 0, so every unscaled form takes one cycle.

OUR ANSWER TO THE QUESTION, AS A PREDICTION

Yes, on E-cores. If the E-core adds only two inputs per cycle, equivalent addresses written in different modes must cost differently: 2 cycles whenever an index and a displacement occur together, 1 otherwise, even when the displacement is 0. The P-core’s 3-input adder predicts no difference for unscaled forms.

This is our hypothesis, before the results. On the E-core, imagine the address adder has two inputs, and the index and displacement compete for the same one. Any two parts fit in one cycle, but index plus displacement needs a second pass. The P-core would use a three-input adder, feeding zero for missing parts. This answers the question on the cover: yes, equivalent addresses can take different time depending on the mode, but only on cores built like this. On E-cores, any address with both an index and a displacement should cost 2 cycles, even when the displacement is zero; on the P-core, every unscaled form should cost 1. A scaled index, times 4 or times 8, is a separate cost: it takes 2 cycles on both P-cores we measured. After the pipeline sketch and our method, the test checks exactly that.

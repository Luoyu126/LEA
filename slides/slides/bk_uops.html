This answers the question timing couldn’t answer: is the slow E-core form two micro-ops, or one micro-op that takes 2 cycles? We counted with Intel VTune, which reads the CPU’s own counters for instructions and micro-ops. A small program ran just one kernel, pinned to one CPU, and we subtracted an empty run on the same CPU to remove start-up and background work. On the Gracemont E-core, lea rax,[rax+rcx+0] is still 1.0 micro-op, the same as without the plus-zero, while the cycle counter shows it takes 2 cycles. So the extra cycle happens inside one micro-op. Two controls bracket that number: add rax,rcx, known to be one micro-op, came out at 1.0, and the 16-bit lea, which really is cracked into several, came out at about 3 with about 2 of them from the microcode ROM. So the method can detect a split, and on the plus-zero form it does not. Numbers are from the i7-13700H, per instruction, mean of 3 runs on 2 CPUs per core type. Single rounds of the one-micro-op rows scatter between 0.91 and 1.19, so the second decimal means nothing, but no round of the slow form came near 2.

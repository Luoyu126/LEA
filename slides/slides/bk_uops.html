Backup, for the question timing couldn’t answer: is the slow E-core form two micro-ops, or one micro-op that takes 2 cycles? We counted with Intel VTune, which reads the CPU’s own counters for instructions and micro-ops. A small program ran just one kernel, pinned to one CPU, and we subtracted an empty run on the same CPU to remove start-up and background work. The control, add rax,rcx, came out at 1.0 micro-op, as it should. On the Gracemont E-core, lea rax,[rax+rcx+0] is still 1.0 micro-op, the same as without the plus-zero, while the cycle counter shows it takes 2 cycles. So the extra cycle happens inside one micro-op. The 16-bit lea is a different story: about 3 micro-ops on the E-core, about 2 of them from the microcode ROM, which is the slow path for unusual instructions, and 2 micro-ops on the P-core. The E-core counts are a bit noisy, about plus or minus 0.1 per run, but that is far too small to confuse 1 with 2.
